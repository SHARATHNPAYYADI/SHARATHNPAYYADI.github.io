Overview

📌 What We Built

A portable, autonomous crop-monitoring device that uses a Convolutional Neural Network to detect two economically destructive paddy diseases — blast and blight — from leaf images in real time, then alerts the farmer via SMS.

Rice blast alone destroys enough rice annually to feed over 60 million people. Bacterial blight can devastate up to 80% of a crop if undetected early. Our system targets detection at the earliest visible symptom stage, when intervention is still effective.

Problem

⚠️ Why This Matters

  • Blast and blight can spread across an entire field within 36 hours of onset
  • Manual inspection across large farm fields is physically infeasible for a single farmer
  • 20–30% of annual paddy yield is destroyed by these two diseases alone
  • Rural areas often lack internet connectivity, ruling out cloud-based solutions
  • Existing detection methods require lab equipment or trained agronomists on-site
Approach

🧠 CNN Model Design

Two separate Keras sequential models were trained — one for blast, one for blight. Each model outputs a confidence score between 0 and 1; a value below 0.5 indicates disease presence. Decision logic then combines both scores to determine the final alert.

Results

📊 Experimental Results

The system was validated using diseased paddy leaf samples collected from Dhamtari district, Chhattisgarh. Both models run simultaneously for each captured image, and the combined decision logic determines the disease type and appropriate remedy.

Rice blast — characteristic grey-white lesions on leaf
Bacterial blight — yellowing and wilting from leaf edges

1 0.548 0.672 Healthy
2 0.620 0.024 Blight
3 5.7e⁻² 0.540 Blast
4 0.360 0.240 Blight
5 0.000 0.000 Both likely

Scores < 0.5 indicate disease presence. When X > Y → Blast detected; when Y < X → Blight detected. SMS alerts include the specific remedy: Nitrogen fertilizer for both blast and blight.

Challenges

🔧 What Was Hard

Deploying Keras model to Raspberry Pi Porting the model trained in Spyder (desktop Python) to the Pi's ARM architecture required resolving library version conflicts and optimizing for limited RAM.
Hardware-software integration Making the servo motor, Pi Camera, GSM module, and CNN inference pipeline run simultaneously and reliably required careful timing and interrupt management.
Servo motor calibration Achieving precise 90° increments for full 360° coverage while keeping power consumption acceptable took significant tuning of the SERVO library parameters.
Future Work

🔮 What's Next
